Combat in RS3 vs OSRS is truly night and day in most regards. We have no weapon attack speed, our prayers aren't 100% effective, special attacks are something to use adrenaline on rather than having a set special attack energy, and much more. In this guide, we will outline the basics of RS3 combat and how to best approach it using language that will be familiar to an OSRS player, while introducing concepts that are unique to RS3

## Weapon Tiers

In OSRS, you will be familiar with weapons having level equipment requirements. This is the exact same in RS3. As an OSRS player, you might have two 75 strength weapons with very different stats, such as an Abyssal Whip and an Abyssal Dagger. In RS3, weapon base strength is uniform across tiers. A Polypore Staff and a Staff of Light are both T75 magic weapons and thus have identical strength. Inside the tooltip, when you hover over it you will find its Accuracy and Ability modifiers.

Melee and Necromancy weapons will have Damage as an innate stat. Magic weapons have no damage, with all of it coming from your spell. Ranged has partial damage from the weapon, and partial damage from ammunition. If a Range weapon supplies its own ammo such as the Crystal Bow, it behaves like a Melee and Necro weapon and applies its own Damage. You can equip damage modifying ammunition such as God Arrows for their benefits. This however does not apply to Dual Wield range weapons that supply their own ammo, similar to how the Tonalztics of Ralos don't benefit from having enchanted bolts equipped.

For the most part, all equal tiered weapons will have the same accuracy and damage stats. Due to Runescape being a quirky game there are exceptions. For the sake of this guide I will refer to these as Misaligned Tiers, a full list of which can be found below. These can range from intentional design that's mentioned in partially (Dragon Rider Lance has T80 Damage and T90 Accuracy, the description only mentions the T90 Accuracy) to fully ignored (Attuned Crystal Weaponry is T80, while possessing a passive that really adds to T82).

All Crafted Weapons (Elder Rune +5, Eternal Magic Longbow, etc.) generally have lower damage and accuracy values than their equipment tier. This as well varies based on weapon tier and the quality of the item. For example a +5 Elder Rune 2H is a T90 in equipping requirement with T85 stats. A +3 Elder Rune 2H will be even less strong. Frankly there are too many to list, but if it can be crafted and can’t be augmented chances are its tier is misaligned. This applies to armor too. Check the wiki for specifics, but if it doesn't have the same damage and accuracy number bonuses as an ACTUAL (T90 or whatever) weapon, it is misaligned.

## Offhand Weapons

Unique to RS3, most Mainhanded Weapons have an Offhand Counterpart! Having both equipped will do full Damage and Accuracy respective to the weapons you have equipped. Removing an Offhand while keeping a Mainhand will do roughly 33% less Damage and Accuracy. The vast majority of players camp weapons and switch briefly to a shield if they need to use Defensive Abilities.

## Shields and Defenders

Much like OSRS, we also use Shields and Defenders. Unlike OSRS, these provide no strength or accuracy bonuses. Instead, they are used as a brief switch if a player needs to use a shield locked defensive ability like Barricade. The higher the shield, the more potent a defensive ability will become. Defenders function as half their tier of shield and offhand at once. For example, a T90 Defender will function as a T45 Shield and a T45 Offhand.

## Attack Speed

In OSRS, you will be familiar with weapons attacking at different speeds. For example, the Abyssal Whip has an Attack Speed of 4 Ticks (2.4s). In RS3, this function is completely removed. Instead, all attacks you deal with abilities are on a 3 Tick cycle (1.8s) also known as the Global Cooldown or GCD. Some items and abilities do not incur this GCD such as the Granite Maul or the Magic ability, Runic Charge.

## Hitchance

Within OSRS, you might be familiar with weapon accuracy, either hitting or not, and “Splashing” where you deliberately lower your Magic stat so you don't kill a target and just cast spells non stop. Splashing is fully removed in RS3. All attacks will never miss a target. However, accuracy is not fully removed. Instead, your accuracy depends on the weapon you have equipped, your level in the combat skill you are using and any stat boosts versus the monster's defense. This accuracy value will be shown on a monster's HP bar as a percentage from 0 to 100. Having 100% accuracy means you will deal 100% of your potential damage. Having 75% accuracy means that you will deal 75% of your potential damage and so on.

## Weaknesses

Inside OSRS you might be familiar with using a weapon such as the Osmumtens Fang against a stab weak enemy for extra accuracy, or with Magic using Water Spells on Dragons to exploit their elemental weakness. The vast majority of PVM encounters do not have any style or weapon specific weaknesses. This will be shown on the monster HP bar, and can be hovered over with the mouse to show it if you don't recognise the icon. Against some creatures that are weak to stab/arrows/spells or whatever, this will only provide an accuracy boost, with no extra damage like elemental weaknesses.

## Armor

Unlike OSRS where you might mix-and-match armors from different styles, or have armors with no negative accuracy penalties, RS3 is style locked. Equipping off-style armor will incur a severe penalty to your accuracy.

Like OSRS, we have both Tank and Power armor. Generally Power armor is preferred, due to the negligible power in comparison to Tank armor. Like weapons, some of the crafted armors can have misaligned tier bonuses. For the most part, focus on armor that can be augmented for defensive perks, and late-game armor that has a passive effect.

## Equipment bonuses

In OSRS, you might be familiar with items such as the Necklace of Anguish, which has both Range strength and range attack bonuses. In RS3, we just have flat style-specific bonuses, with accuracy on some late-game amulets as a passive effect. All power armor has damage bonuses as well. Compared to OSRS where a max hit is a big deal, all RS3 damage bonuses add their value to your overall max hit. For example, an Amulet of Fury has +37.3 damage bonus. This would literally add 37.3 damage to your base ability damage.

## Prayer

In OSRS, prayers normally block 100% of damage taken, with exceptions from bosses that do “chip damage” like Akha in Tombs of Amascut. In RS3, chip damage is universal. All bosses will deal half their damage if you are praying against their specific attacks. Additionally, certain items such as the Amulet of Souls bump this to 60% protection. Certain abilities such as Devotion make this 100% temporarily, and certain perks such as Devoted or Enhanced Devoted give a random chance to activate 100% protection for a short period.

Prayer flicking as you know it in OSRS is largely removed. You can not “1 tick flick” a prayer to preserve prayer points, or “lazy flick” a DPS prayer to achieve the same result. Instead, DPS prayers are camped. If an RS3 player refers to prayer flicking, they are referring to switching prayers like how a player does at Jad or Leviathan. In addition, we have a 2nd prayer book, the Ancient Curses. These are overcharged variants of the Normal prayers. Sap and Leech curses can be activated at once, but the most powerful is the Torment tier. As well, you can unlock various prayers from bosses including T99 ones from Nex: Angel of Death, and two new ones for the Normal Book. There is a Protect/Deflect prayer against Necromancy, and Soul Split, which is often flicked with protection prayers.

## Special Attacks

Finally we come to the largest difference in combat, the switch from the Special Attack energy system to adrenaline. Instead of starting at 100%, a player starts at 0% adren and builds this through using “Basic Abilities". You can spend this adrenaline on "Threshold Abilities" and “Ultimate Abilities" to bolster your combat ability. Additionally, weapon specific special attacks are largely retained. However, unlike OSRS where you'd bring something like Dragon Claws or a similar special attack weapon despite it being a lower tier weapon, RS3 this does not occur. There is an extremely powerful item called an “Essence of Finalty” where you can store any weapon with a special attack inside it to get the same power of your main weapon. For example, a pair of Dragon Claws can be stored inside an EOF and cast while you have a T90 weapon equipped like Drygores or a Noxious Scythe.
