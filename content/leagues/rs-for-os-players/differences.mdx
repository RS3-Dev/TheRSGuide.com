## Differences between OSRS and RS3

RS3 also of course comes with a very long list of differences compared to OSRS; so long that this page can’t even begin to cover all of them. Therefore, this page aims to cover relatively surface level differences that shape the game to be fundamentally different from OSRS. This page will go over key differences in Interface, Skills, Content, and finally Combat; which will be covered in its own dedicated page.

## Interface

### Customization

To begin, right away you’ll notice the interface and the amount of systems being shown on your screen is already significantly different than OSRS. The base UI of the game is (mostly) customizable without the need of something like RuneLite and you’ll notice you can have many different windows open at a given time rather than having to cycle a menu through to change what you’re seeing on your interface. Specific details on customizing this can be found in the [Interface](https://thersguide.com/setup/interface-setup) section.

### Combat and General Interface

Right away you might be looking for your HP and prayer near your minimap, as that would be intuitive from an OSRS perspective; however in RS3, things like your HP and Prayer are instead shown on their own dedicated frame. You’ll also notice that there are bars where you can place combat abilities, items, prayers, and spells; of which you can also keybind individually. So if you’d like to swap between praying Melee/Mage/Range, you can set keybinds for these rather than clicking them in the Prayer book, and you can always have them shown in a place of your choosing on your interface. As for details on combat abilities, different items used in PvM, what prayers are useful, and what spells you should be concerned with are all covered in the [Action Bar](https://thersguide.com/setup/action-bar), [Combat Basics](https://thersguide.com/getting-started/combat-basics), [Prayers](https://thersguide.com/getting-started/prayers-curses), [Buff Bar and Debuff Bar](https://thersguide.com/setup/buff-bar), and other related pages.

Of course along with this there are also many different menus that are not present or are very different compared to OSRS. The grouping system has a different window with different options under the Social tab, as well as being able to guest in Clan chats from here. RS3 has a Bestiary where you can see information about different bosses which shows killcount, pr’s, and links to collection logs as well as combat achievements and details about each boss.

### XP Rates and Methods

It’s worth mentioning that many of the metas in OSRS skilling don’t necessarily carry over to RS3. Things like 2t or 3t Fishing, 2t or 1.5t Woodcutting, and 3t or 4t Mining etc; don’t really apply here due to systemic differences in the way these skills function. Similar can be said about Cooking, but instead of 2t we have 3t. There are still ways to maximize xp/h when it comes to tick manipulation, however many guides and xp rates don’t account for this as most RS3 content and guides are catered toward more afk style play rather than having a lot of different inputs you can exploit for faster rates. Part of this is due to there being more systems and tools in RS3 to incentivize or assist in afk training. *Most RS3 training guides will simply give a level range to train at and assume afk.*

XP rates are also generally high across the board in most cases, most noticeably in the early game. This won’t change your gameplay as much as you might think, but it does speed up the early game progression. *(If you are playing RS3 solely in a Leagues environment, this is much less relevant due to the massive XP rate increases).*

## Content

RS3, being developed for many years after the original base for OSRS was built off of, comes with various forms of new content differing from OSRS. While OSRS has its own additions like the Theatre of Blood, RS3 has its own as well ranging from PvM content to Skilling content and Quests/Regions.

### The Sixth Age

Much of RS3’s differing content takes place in what is now the Sixth Age, rather than the Fifth Age you might be accustomed to in OSRS. This age shift in the lore is marked by the quest [The World Wakes](https://runescape.wiki/w/The_World_Wakes) (spoilers). Therefore, much of the lore and content released after this point has Gielinor in a very different world state than you might be used to, featuring many Gods like Saradomin and Zamorak as returning characters to the story. Due to this, some content additions like the Heart of Gielinor (also known as GWD2) and the island of Anachronia do not have equivalents in OSRS that you would be able to recognize. Some content before The World Wakes is also unique to RS3, such as the addition of the Ancient Curses as an alternative to the standard Prayers (similar to the Ruinous Powers from OSRS Leagues); so while it isn’t a perfect dividing line for content that OSRS and RS3 share, it can be a good indicator for the “new” and the “old”. Quests will state what year they were released in the top right of the quest preview, and anything past 2007 of course; is going to be content that OSRS most likely does not have an equivalent for, with some exceptions like While Guthix Sleeps. *(If you are playing RS3 solely in a Leagues environment; you may not interact with many of these quests as they may be auto completed, but just be aware of the diverging story and lore path RS3 is on compared to OSRS).*

### Quests

Also on the topic of quests, many famous quests such as Demon Slayer and Romeo & Juliet are either significantly changed or replaced altogether. On top of this, some questlines deviate in their path such as the Elf quest series ending with Song of the Elves to gain access to Prif, while RS3 instead grants access through the quest Plague’s End and then continues afterward into the Sixth Age. Some quest expansions that exist in OSRS like DT2, are also not present in RS3 as they were developed around being a storyline set in the Fifth Age; following a different story progression. The exact differences between quests are far too much for this page to cover by itself, but just know that while there are some shared quests between the games; they are not always going to be a 1:1 copy and will usually have some varying differences in content or even rewards. *(A side note: at this current point in time there is no full equivalent to Quest Helper, although it is in the process of being added as an available feature. For now, guides and the wiki are your go-to for quests).*

### The Wilderness

One of the most noticeable differences for OSRS players is that the Wilderness in RS3 does not have forced PvP by default; instead having it off with an opt-in feature to have the always on PvP should you desire that. (Should you opt-in to Wilderness PvP and die, similar punishments do incur where you will lose the majority of your items and only keep certain Quest related items). Due to this, the Wilderness is obviously far less dangerous due to the lower risk of pk’ing and is treated more like other regions than it is in OSRS. The Wilderness in RS3 differs heavily in terms of Slayer monsters, Quests, and even features extra content such as Dungeoneering and 2 Elite Dungeons which are technically considered to be part of the Wilderness content. It’s also worth noting no items will be deleted beyond the Level 20 Wilderness either. Basically, for all intents and purposes treat it like a normal region in the game.

### Achievement Diaries

Another difference is Achievement Diaries are renamed to Area Tasks in RS3. Much of the functionality is preserved here, just with a different interface and many of the rewards are modified; such as Morytania not giving a Barrows rune drop buff but buffing Blood Rune crafting instead. While many of the Diary tasks are shared or at least similar, many of them differ; especially in the higher tier tasks. Any routes you might be following will usually account for Diaries when relevant and you can always check their task lists and rewards in the Achievements > Area Tasks section in your UI.

### Logout Timer

Lastly, in what is possibly a gameplay shifting difference to many dedicated players is the fact that RS3 does not have a 6h logout timer. This means (so long as you don’t afk for more than 15m with no inputs) you can stay logged in as long as you’d like without being forcibly disconnected. Due to this, that also means there isn’t really such a thing as a “6h log” in RS3 as there is no such thing as a 6h window to begin with. This is a completely foreign concept to RS3 players and you’ll pretty much never see this mentioned.
